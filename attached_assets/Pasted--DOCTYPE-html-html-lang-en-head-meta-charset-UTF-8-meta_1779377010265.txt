    <p>"Bhastrika" means bellows — the blacksmith's tool that fans fire. Often confused with Kapalabhati, but they are different. In Bhastrika, <strong>both the inhale and the exhale are forceful and equal</strong>. It is more intense than Kapalabhati and generates more heat.</p>
    <h3>The Technique</h3>
    <ul>
      <li>Sit upright. Take a deep breath in to prepare.</li>
      <li><strong>Forceful exhale</strong> through the nose — push the air out completely, belly contracts in.</li>
      <li><strong>Forceful inhale</strong> through the nose — pull the air in powerfully, belly expands out.</li>
      <li>Both are active, equal, and driven by the diaphragm and abdomen. Think of it like pumping a bellows — vigorous in both directions.</li>
      <li><strong>Arms (optional but traditional):</strong> On the inhale, raise both arms above your head. On the exhale, bring them down sharply to shoulder height, elbows bent. This arm movement amplifies the breath and energy movement through the body.</li>
      <li>Speed: Medium — about one full breath cycle per 1–2 seconds. Not as rapid as Kapalabhati.</li>
    </ul>
    <h3>A Round</h3>
    <p>10–20 forceful breath cycles. After the last exhale, take one deep, slow inhale and hold briefly. Exhale completely. Rest for 30 seconds. Repeat for 3 rounds. Build intensity gradually.</p>
    <div class="tip"><p><strong>Benefits:</strong> Generates significant internal heat, clears energy blockages, increases lung capacity, stimulates the nervous system, and builds enormous vitality (prana). Considered one of the most powerful pranayamas for energy and purification. Many advanced yogis use Bhastrika before meditation.</p></div>
    <div class="warn"><p><strong>Caution:</strong> Not for beginners to do intensely. Do not practice if pregnant, with high blood pressure, heart conditions, hernia, vertigo, or epilepsy. Always on an empty stomach. Stop if you feel faint or nauseated.</p></div>

    <h2>Sitali — Cooling Breath</h2>
    <p>"Sitali" means cool or cold. One of the only pranayamas that inhales through the mouth. By drawing air over a curled, moist tongue — like sipping through a straw — the air is cooled before entering the body. Cooling, calming, and grounding.</p>
    <h3>The Technique</h3>
    <ul>
      <li>Sit upright, eyes closed.</li>
      <li>Open your mouth into an "O" shape. <strong>Curl your tongue lengthwise</strong> into a tube — like a rolled leaf or a straw. Stick it out slightly past your lips. (Not everyone can curl their tongue — it is genetic. If you can't, use Sitkari instead: gently press teeth together, part lips, inhale through the teeth with a soft hiss.)</li>
      <li><strong>Inhale slowly and deeply through the curled tongue.</strong> Feel the cool, moist air rolling across it. Fill belly first, then chest.</li>
      <li><strong>Retract your tongue, close your mouth.</strong></li>
      <li><strong>Exhale slowly and completely through the nose.</strong></li>
      <li>Repeat 5–15 times. Build to 5–10 minutes.</li>

Also reduces hunger, thirst, anger, high blood pressure, and fever. Best practiced in hot weather, after heating practices like Bhastrika, or when you feel overstimulated.</p>
    <div class="warn"><p><strong>Do not practice</strong> in cold weather or polluted air — the air bypasses the nose's natural filter and warmer. Avoid with low blood pressure or respiratory conditions.</p></div>

    <h2>Murcha — Swooning Breath</h2>
    <p>"Murcha" means fainting or swooning. Mentioned in the Hatha Yoga Pradipika. This advanced technique uses breath retention with the chin lock (Jalandhara Bandha) to deliberately induce a state between conscious and unconscious — a controlled, blissful dissolution of the ego-mind. Not for beginners.</p>
    <h3>The Technique</h3>
    <ul>
      <li>Sit in a comfortable meditative posture, spine straight. Take a few normal breaths.</li>
      <li><strong>Inhale deeply and slowly through the nose</strong> — long, steady, at least 5–8 seconds. Fill the lungs completely.</li>
      <li>At the top of the inhale, <strong>apply Jalandhara Bandha</strong>: lengthen the back of the neck and bring the chin slightly toward the chest — about 1–2 inches, not all the way. This creates a gentle lock at the throat that controls the energy.</li>
      <li><strong>Hold the breath inside</strong> (Antara Kumbhaka) for as long as comfortable — 5, 10, 20 seconds. Some teachers also add: roll the eyes gently upward toward the third eye (Shambhavi Mudra). Focus awareness at the Ajna chakra (point between eyebrows).</li>
      <li><strong>Release the chin lock</strong> first. Then <strong>exhale very slowly through the nose</strong> — gently at first, slightly more forcefully toward the end to fully empty the lungs.</li>
      <li>Rest. Breathe normally. Observe the stillness. That is one round. Repeat 3–8 times.</li>
    </ul>
    <h3>What You'll Experience</h3>
    <p>Light-headedness. A floating sensation. Mental stillness — the mind quiets in a way that is distinctly different from other practices. Warmth or tingling in the skull and third eye area. With longer practice, a state approaching trance. The Hatha Yoga Pradipika says it "causes the mind to swoon and gives comfort."</p>
    <div class="warn"><p><strong>Advanced practice — required guidance for first sessions.</strong> Do not do if you have high blood pressure, heart conditions, epilepsy, neck injuries, or glaucoma. Never push to actual fainting. If dizzy, stop and breathe normally. Empty stomach only.</p></div>

    <h2>Nine Purification Breaths</h2>
    <p>From the Bön tradition of Tibet — the indigenous spiritual lineage that predates Buddhism and co-evolved with it. Called <em>Lung Ro Gu Chu Dupa</em>. This is the foundational preliminary practice done before any meditation or advanced Tibetan practice. Nine breaths, three sets of three, each clearing one of the three root obstacles: anger, attachment, and ignorance.</p>
    <h3>Setup</h3>
    <p>Sit upright — full lotus or cross-legged. Spine straight, shoulders open, hands in equipoise (palms up, left resting on right, thumbs touching). Chin slightly in. Visualize three channels in the body: <strong>right (white)</strong>, <strong>left (red)</strong>, and <strong>central (blue)</strong> — all meeting about four fingers below the navel, the central one rising to the crown of the head.</p>
    <h3>Set 1 — Clearing Anger (Right/White Channel)</h3>
    <ul>
      <li>Right hand up: close <strong>right nostril</strong> with right ring finger.</li>
      <li><strong>Inhale slowly through the left nostril.</strong> Visualize pure air as green light filling the left (red) channel all the way down to the navel junction.</li>
      <li>Switch: close left nostril, open right.</li>
      <li><strong>Exhale through the right nostril</strong> — gently at first, more forcefully at the end. Visualize anger leaving as dark smoke through the right channel, dissolving into space.</li>
      <li>Repeat 3 times total.</li>
    </ul>
    <h3>Set 2 — Clearing Attachment (Left/Red Channel)</h3>
    <ul>
      <li>Left hand up: close <strong>left nostril</strong> with left ring finger.</li>
      <li><strong>Inhale through the right nostril.</strong> Visualize pure light filling the white right channel to the navel.</li>
      <li>Switch: close right nostril.</li>
      <li><strong>Exhale through the left nostril.</strong> Attachment and desire leave as dark smoke, dissolve into space.</li>
      <li>Repeat 3 times total.</li>
    </ul>
    <h3>Set 3 — Clearing Ignorance (Central/Blue Channel)</h3>
    <ul>
      <li>Both hands down. Breathe through <strong>both nostrils simultaneously.</strong></li>
      <li><strong>Inhale through both nostrils.</strong> Visualize pure light filling the central blue channel from the navel upward.</li>
      <li><strong>Exhale through both nostrils</strong> — gently, then more forcefully at the end. Ignorance, doubt, and lack of confidence leave through the crown of the head as dark smoke, dissolving into boundless space.</li>
      <li>Repeat 3 times total.</li>
    </ul>
    <p><strong>After all nine breaths:</strong> Rest. Feel all three channels as clear, open, and luminous. Sit in the openness before continuing to meditation.</p>
    <div class="tip"><p>This is applied neuroregulation as much as spiritual practice — each set activates different vagal pathways. The visualization is not decoration; it directs the nervous system's response. Do not skip it.</p></div>

    <h2>Physiological Sigh — Double Inhale</h2>
    <p>Researched and popularized by Dr. Andrew Huberman and the Stanford Neuroscience lab. A 2023 randomized controlled trial found it was <strong>the single most effective breathing technique</strong> for reducing acute stress — outperforming mindfulness meditation, box breathing, and cyclic hyperventilation. It works in as little as one breath.</p>
    <h3>The Technique</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>First inhale:</strong> Breathe in deeply through the nose. Fill the lungs most of the way — about 80%.</li>
      <li><strong>Second inhale:</strong> Without exhaling, take one more sharp sniff through the nose to <em>top off</em> the lungs completely. This re-inflates any collapsed alveoli (tiny air sacs that partially collapse under stress, causing that panicked "I can't breathe" feeling).</li>
      <li><strong>Long, slow exhale:</strong> Release all the air through your mouth — slowly, completely, for as long as possible. This activates the vagus nerve and parasympathetic nervous system maximally.</li>
      <li>That's it. One to three of these and you will feel a shift.</li>
    </ul>
    <h3>How to Use It</h3>
    <p>This is your emergency tool. Not a timed session. Use it: the moment stress spikes, when a craving hits, when you feel overwhelmed, in traffic, before walking into a difficult situation, mid-panic attack. Because it is so simple, you can do it invisibly — no one around you will notice.</p>
    <div class="tip"><p><strong>The science:</strong> The double inhale maximally inflates the alveoli and increases the surface area for CO₂ transfer. The long exhale then offloads maximum CO₂, which is the actual trigger for the calming response — not oxygen. Stanford found 5 minutes of cyclic sighing daily produced the largest mood improvements of any breathwork protocol tested.</p></div>

    <h2>Rebirthing Breathwork</h2>
    <p>Developed by Leonard Orr in the 1970s — the grandfather of the modern Western breathwork movement. All major breathwork lineages trace back to him: Holotropic Breathwork (Stanislav Grof), Transformational Breath (Judith Kravitz), Liberation Breathing (Sondra Ray). Orr discovered the technique while breathing in his bathtub and experiencing what he described as re-living his own birth. The method uses <strong>circular connected breathing</strong> — no pause between inhale and exhale, ever — to access suppressed memories, release stored trauma, and reach states of expanded consciousness.</p>
    <h3>The Core Technique — Circular Connected Breathing</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Full inhale:</strong> Deep belly-to-chest breath through the nose (or mouth). Belly expands fully.</li>
      <li><strong>Relaxed exhale:</strong> Let the air fall out — gravity, not effort. No pushing. No pause after exhale.</li>
      <li>Immediately begin the next inhale. <strong>The breath is one continuous connected loop.</strong> In… out… in… out… no stops, no gaps.</li>
      <li>Maintain this rhythm for 45–90 minutes in a proper session. Speed and depth adjust intuitively.</li>
    </ul>
    <h3>What Happens</h3>
    <p>Within 15–20 minutes, altered consciousness states emerge. Physical sensations — tingling, tetany (muscle contractions), warmth or cold. Emotional releases: tears, laughter, grief, joy. Sometimes visual phenomena or memories that surface. This is the material being released. A trained facilitator is present to guide and support.</p>
    <div class="warn"><p><strong>This is not a solo beginner practice.</strong> The traditional method is done one-on-one with a trained Rebirther for a series of 10 sessions. The altered states can be intense. Not recommended for people with psychosis, severe trauma without therapeutic support, or cardiovascular conditions. Find a certified practitioner at rebirthingbreathwork.com</p></div>

    <h2>Reverse Breathing — Taoist / Qigong</h2>
    <p>Also called Taoist Breathing, Paradoxical Breathing, or Back-to-the-Womb Breathing. The exact opposite of natural diaphragmatic breathing. Where natural breathing expands the belly on the inhale, reverse breathing <strong>contracts it</strong>. This compresses the internal organs, builds internal pressure in the lower Dan Tian (energy center below the navel), and is said to powerfully concentrate and direct Qi (life force). Used in Tai Chi, Qigong, and traditional Chinese martial arts.</p>
    <h3>The Technique</h3>
    <ul>
      <li>Sit or stand in a relaxed, upright position. Take a few natural breaths to settle.</li>
      <li><strong>Inhale through the nose:</strong> Gently <strong>pull the lower abdomen inward and upward</strong> — draw the belly button toward the spine. Simultaneously, allow the chest and upper ribcage to expand. The perineum (pelvic floor) lightly contracts upward. This feels unnatural at first — practice slowly.</li>
      <li><strong>Exhale through the nose:</strong> Release the belly — let it expand outward and downward. The chest and ribcage soften and release. Perineum relaxes.</li>
      <li>Build the practice gradually. Start with 5–10 breaths. Increase to 10-minute sessions over weeks.</li>
    </ul>
    <h3>What It Does</h3>
    <p>Creates a compression-and-release pump that massages the internal organs and builds internal energy pressure (Qi). In Chinese medicine, this is how Qi is "stored" and made powerful. Used before Tai Chi and martial arts practice to "charge" the body. Also builds core stability, improves digestion, and increases bodily awareness. The sensation of qi moving — warmth, tingling, fullness — is often reported.</p>
    <div class="tip"><p><strong>Start very slowly.</strong> The tendency is to use too much effort. The contraction should be gentle, not forced. Over time it becomes natural and effortless. Combine with visualization of warm golden light filling the Dan Tian (three fingers below the navel) to amplify the effect.</p></div>

    <h2>Kumbhaka — Breath Retention</h2>
    <p>"Kumbha" means pot or vessel. Kumbhaka is the deliberate suspension of breath — either after full inhalation (<em>Antara Kumbhaka</em>, inner retention) or after full exhalation (<em>Bahya Kumbhaka</em>, outer retention). In ancient yogic texts, Kumbhaka is described as the most powerful practice of all pranayama — the moment when prana (life force) is "trapped" in the body and most powerfully directed. Most advanced pranayamas (Murcha, Tummo, advanced Nadi Shodhana) use Kumbhaka as their core mechanism.</p>
    <h3>Antara Kumbhaka — Inner Retention (hold after inhale)</h3>
    <ul>
      <li>Inhale fully and deeply through the nose.</li>
      <li>At the top of the breath, <strong>gently close the throat</strong> (not forcefully — just a soft seal, like stopping a yawn). Do not tense the neck or face.</li>
      <li>Hold for a count that is comfortable — 4 seconds, then 8, then 16 as you build.</li>
      <li>Exhale slowly and completely through the nose.</li>
    </ul>
    <h3>Bahya Kumbhaka — Outer Retention (hold after exhale)</h3>
    <ul>
      <li>Exhale fully and completely through the nose — empty the lungs as much as possible.</li>
      <li>After the exhale, <strong>hold the empty lungs</strong>. This is where Uddiyana Bandha (abdominal lock — pull belly up and in) can be applied for deeper effect.</li>
      <li>Hold for a comfortable count — start with 4 seconds.</li>
      <li>Inhale slowly when the urge to breathe arises. Never force.</li>
    </ul>
    <h3>What It Does</h3>
    <p>Inner retention builds prana, raises internal pressure, and is energizing. Outer retention releases and empties, deeply calms the nervous system, and increases CO₂ tolerance. Both improve lung capacity, focus, and the depth of meditation that follows. Kumbhaka is why Wim Hof's retention works — the same principle, taken to an extreme.</p>
    <div class="warn"><p><strong>Never practice Bahya Kumbhaka if you have high blood pressure, heart disease, or are pregnant.</strong> Never force either form of retention — the hold ends when the first urge to breathe arises, not when you push past it. Practice seated.</p></div>

    <h2>Huberman Blink Protocols</h2>
    <p>Dr. Andrew Huberman (Stanford Neuroscience) identified that blinking is not just eye lubrication — it is a neurological control switch. Each blink acts like a shutter that resets your brain's time perception and attention. You can deliberately use blink rate to shift your mental state in either direction: toward sharp focus or toward deep rest.</p>

    <h3>Protocol 1 — Fast Blinks: Boost Alertness &amp; Focus</h3>
    <p>Rapid blinking increases dopamine signaling and speeds up the brain's "frame rate" — how finely it slices time. More dopamine = more blinks = sharper attention.</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Sit upright. Relax your face.</li>
      <li><strong>Blink rapidly</strong> — 1 blink per second or faster — for <strong>30–60 seconds.</strong></li>
      <li>Your eyes may water slightly. That is normal and beneficial — it re-lubricates the eyes and resets the visual system.</li>
      <li>After the rapid blinks, <strong>open your eyes wide</strong> and fix your gaze softly on a point in front of you for 30 seconds.</li>
      <li>Notice the shift in mental clarity.</li>
    </ul>
    <div class="tip"><p><strong>Use when:</strong> You feel mentally foggy, drowsy, or unfocused. Before starting work, teaching, or any task requiring sharp attention. Takes under 2 minutes.</p></div>

    <h3>Protocol 2 — Slow Blinks: Downshift to Rest &amp; Sleep</h3>
    <p>Slow deliberate blinks mimic the brain's natural pre-sleep behavior. Each long blink reduces the flow of visual information to the brain — telling the nervous system it is safe to power down.</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Sit or lie down. Soften your body.</li>
      <li><strong>Blink slowly and deliberately</strong> — close your eyes fully, hold for 1–2 seconds, open slowly. One blink every 3–5 seconds.</li>
      <li>Continue for <strong>2–5 minutes.</strong></li>
      <li>With each slow blink, consciously let your shoulders drop and your jaw unclench.</li>
      <li>Combine with slow nasal breathing — in 4s, out 6s — for maximum effect.</li>
    </ul>
    <div class="tip"><p><strong>Use when:</strong> Preparing for sleep. After a stressful event. To transition out of screen time. Combine with Bhramari or Chandra Bhedana for a powerful pre-sleep stack.</p></div>

    <h3>Protocol 3 — The 17-Minute Attentional Blink Training</h3>
    <p>A more intensive protocol based on Huberman's discussion of attentional blink research. The goal is to <strong>permanently reduce attentional lapses</strong> — the small gaps in awareness where your brain misses things because it was momentarily offline.</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Sit quietly. No distractions. Set a timer for 17 minutes.</li>
      <li>Pick a fixed point to look at — a dot on a wall, a word on a page.</li>
      <li><strong>Maintain soft visual focus</strong> on that point. Do not stare intensely — soft gaze, relaxed face.</li>
      <li>When your attention drifts (and it will), simply notice and return. Do not judge. The returning is the training.</li>
      <li>Blink naturally throughout — do not suppress blinking. Let the blinks happen on their own rhythm.</li>
      <li>The 17 minutes is not arbitrary — research suggests this is the minimum duration for the practice to begin rewiring attentional circuits.</li>
    </ul>
    <div class="tip"><p>Huberman notes that this practice compounds over weeks. Daily 17-minute sessions measurably reduce the frequency and duration of attentional blinks — meaning your brain misses less, catches more, and stays online longer under cognitively demanding conditions.</p></div>

The energy does not fall back down — it remains centripetal, circulating within.</p>

// ── Pump engine (Kapalabhati / Bhastrika) ─────────
function startPump(){
  ensureAC(); closeInfo();
  totalSecs=getDur(); elapsedTotal=0; running=true; btnBegin.textContent='Stop';
  const isBhastrika=(tech==='bhastrika');
  const pumpInterval=isBhastrika ? 1500 : 700; // ms per breath
  let pumpCount=0;
  const startTime=performance.now();
  setPC('p-fire');

  function loop(){
    if(!running) return;
    const now=performance.now();
    elapsedTotal=(now-startTime)/1000;
    const totalRemain=Math.max(0,totalSecs-elapsedTotal);

    if(elapsedTotal>=totalSecs){
      rPhase.textContent='Complete'; rCount.textContent=''; rInfo.textContent='';
      setFill(1); setPC(null); doneTone(); recordSession(tech, Math.round(totalSecs));
      running=false; btnBegin.textContent='Begin'; return;
    }

    const cycleT=pumpInterval/1000;
    const cyclePos=(elapsedTotal%cycleT)/cycleT;
    const isExhale=cyclePos<0.5;

    if(isExhale){
      rPhase.textContent=isBhastrika?'Force Out':'Sharp Out';
      setPC('p-fire');
    } else {
      rPhase.textContent=isBhastrika?'Force In':'Passive In';
      setPC('p-inhale');
    }

    // Count pumps
    const newCount=Math.floor(elapsedTotal/(cycleT));
    if(newCount>pumpCount){ pumpCount=newCount; pumpTone(); }

    rCount.textContent=pumpCount+1;
    rInfo.textContent=fmtTime(totalRemain)+' left';
    setFill(cyclePos);

    raf=requestAnimationFrame(loop);
  }
  loop();
}

function startBhramari(){
  ensureAC(); closeInfo();
  totalSecs=getDur(); elapsedTotal=0; running=true; btnBegin.textContent='Stop';
  // Phases: inhale 4s, exhale-hum variable (6s)
  const phases=[
    {name:'Inhale',s:4,cls:'p-inhale',snd:'inhale'},
    {name:'Exhale — Hmmmmm',s:6,cls:'p-exhale',snd:null,hum:true},
  ];
  let pi=0; phaseStart=performance.now(); lastTick=-1;
  S.inhale();
  setPC(phases[0].cls); rPhase.textContent=phases[0].name;

  function loop(){
    if(!running) return;
    const now=performance.now();
    const pe=(now-phaseStart)/1000;
    const phase=phases[pi];
    elapsedTotal+=(now-(loop._last||now))/1000; loop._last=now;

    rCount.textContent=Math.ceil(Math.max(0,phase.s-pe));
    rInfo.textContent=fmtTime(totalRemain)+' left';
    setFill(Math.min(1,pe/phase.s));

    if(pe>=phase.s){
      pi=(pi+1)%phases.length;
      phaseStart=performance.now(); lastTick=-1;
      const np=phases[pi];
      if(np.hum) hum(np.s); else if(np.snd) S[np.snd]();
      setPC(np.cls); rPhase.textContent=np.name;
    }
    if(elapsedTotal>=totalSecs){
      rPhase.textContent='Complete'; rCount.textContent=''; rInfo.textContent='';
      setFill(1); setPC(null); doneTone(); recordSession(tech, 0);
      running=false; btnBegin.textContent='Begin'; return;

    raf=requestAnimationFrame(loop);
  }
  loop._last=performance.now();
  raf=requestAnimationFrame(loop);

function startDuration(){
  ensureAC(); closeInfo();
  totalSecs=getDur(); elapsedTotal=0; running=true; btnBegin.textContent='Stop';
  const phases=getPhases(); let pi=0;
  phaseStart=performance.now(); lastTick=-1;

  const useNostrils=NOSTRIL_TECHS.includes(tech);
  showNostrils(useNostrils);

  if(phases[0].snd) S[phases[0].snd]();
  setPC(phases[0].cls); rPhase.textContent=phases[0].name;
  if(useNostrils&&phases[0].nos) setNostrils(phases[0].nos.l,phases[0].nos.r);

  function loop(){
    if(!running) return;
    const now=performance.now();
    const pe=(now-phaseStart)/1000;
    const phase=phases[pi];
    elapsedTotal+=(now-(loop._last||now))/1000; loop._last=now;
    const totalRemain=Math.max(0,totalSecs-elapsedTotal);

    rCount.textContent=Math.ceil(Math.max(0,phase.s-pe));
    rInfo.textContent=fmtTime(totalRemain)+' left';
    setFill(Math.min(1,pe/phase.s));

    const isHold=phase.cls==='p-hold'||phase.cls==='p-hold2';
    const secL=Math.ceil(Math.max(0,phase.s-pe));
    if(isHold&&secL!==lastTick&&secL>0&&secL<phase.s){ tick(); lastTick=secL; }
    else if(!isHold) lastTick=-1;

    if(pe>=phase.s){
      pi=(pi+1)%phases.length;
      phaseStart=performance.now(); lastTick=-1;
      const np=phases[pi];
      if(np.hum) hum(np.s); else if(np.snd) S[np.snd]();
      setPC(np.cls); rPhase.textContent=np.name;
      if(useNostrils&&np.nos) setNostrils(np.nos.l,np.nos.r);
    }
    if(elapsedTotal>=totalSecs){
      rPhase.textContent='Complete'; rCount.textContent=''; rInfo.textContent='';
      setFill(1); setPC(null); showNostrils(false); doneTone(); recordSession(tech, Math.round(totalSecs));
      running=false; btnBegin.textContent='Begin'; return;
    }
    raf=requestAnimationFrame(loop);
  }
  loop._last=performance.now();
  raf=requestAnimationFrame(loop);
}

  powerBreathTone(); setPC('p-fire');
  const start=performance.now();

// ── Heatmap ────────────────────────────────────────
function renderHeatmap() {
  const section = document.getElementById('heatmapSection');
  if (!section) return;
  const sess = loadS();

  // Build date -> total minutes map
  const dayMap = {};
  sess.forEach(function(s) {
    if (!dayMap[s.date]) dayMap[s.date] = 0;
    var mins = s.dur > 0 ? s.dur / 60 : 5;
    dayMap[s.date] += mins;
  });

  // 90 days back from today
  var today = new Date();
  var todayStr = todayS();
  var days = [];
  for (var i = 89; i >= 0; i--) {
    var d = new Date(today);
    d.setDate(d.getDate() - i);
    var dateStr = d.getFullYear() + '-' +
      String(d.getMonth()+1).padStart(2,'0') + '-' +
      String(d.getDate()).padStart(2,'0');
    var mins = dayMap[dateStr] || 0;
    var level = 0;
    if (mins > 0 && mins < 5)   level = 1;
    else if (mins < 10)          level = 2;
    else if (mins < 15)          level = 3;
    else if (mins < 30)          level = 4;
    else if (mins >= 30)         level = 5;
    days.push({ date: dateStr, mins: Math.round(mins), level,
      isToday: dateStr === todayStr,
      month: d.getMonth(), dow: d.getDay() });
  }

  // Group into weeks
  var weeks = [];
  var week = new Array(days[0].dow).fill(null);
  days.forEach(function(day) {
    week.push(day);
    if (week.length === 7) { weeks.push(week); week = []; }
  });
  if (week.length > 0) {
    while (week.length < 7) week.push(null);
    weeks.push(week);
  }

  var monthNames = ['Jan','Feb','Mar','Apr','May','Jun','Jul','Aug','Sep','Oct','Nov','Dec'];
  var monthLabels = new Array(weeks.length).fill('');
  var lastMonth = -1;
  weeks.forEach(function(wk, wi) {
    var firstReal = wk.find(function(d) { return d; });
    if (!firstReal) return;
    if (firstReal.month !== lastMonth) {
      monthLabels[wi] = monthNames[firstReal.month];
      lastMonth = firstReal.month;
    }
  });

  var html2 = '<div class="heatmap-title"><span>Activity &mdash; 3 months</span></div>';
  html2 += '<div class="heatmap-scroll"><div class="heatmap-grid">';

  weeks.forEach(function(wk, wi) {
    html2 += '<div class="heatmap-month-col">';
    html2 += '<div class="heatmap-month-label">' + (monthLabels[wi] || '') + '</div>';
    html2 += '<div class="heatmap-week">';
    wk.forEach(function(day) {
      if (!day) {
        html2 += '<div class="hm-day hm-empty"></div>';
      } else {
        var ring = day.isToday ? ' style="box-shadow:0 0 0 1.5px var(--gold);"' : '';
        var tip = day.mins > 0 ? day.date + ': ' + day.mins + ' min' : day.date + ': rest day';
        html2 += '<div class="hm-day hm-' + day.level + '"' + ring +
          ' data-tip="' + tip + '"' +
          ' onmouseenter="showTip(event,this.dataset.tip)"' +
          ' onmouseleave="hideTip()"></div>';
      }
    });
    html2 += '</div></div>';
  });

  html2 += '</div></div>';

  var labels = ['1-4 min','5-9 min','10-14 min','15-29 min','30+ min'];
  html2 += '<div class="heatmap-legend"><span>0</span>';
  for (var l = 1; l <= 5; l++) {
    html2 += '<div class="hm-leg hm-' + l + '" title="' + labels[l-1] + '"></div>';
  }
  html2 += '<span>30+ min</span></div>';

  section.innerHTML = html2;
}

function renderHistory() {
  const sess = loadS();
  const body = document.getElementById('historyBody');
  const empty = document.getElementById('historyEmpty');
  const filtersEl = document.getElementById('historyFilters');

  // Build filter buttons from unique techniques used
  const techs = [...new Set(sess.map(s => s.tech))];
  filtersEl.innerHTML = '';
  const allBtn = document.createElement('button');
  allBtn.className = 'hf-btn' + (historyFilter === 'all' ? ' on' : '');
  allBtn.textContent = 'All';
  allBtn.onclick = () => { historyFilter = 'all'; renderHistory(); };
  filtersEl.appendChild(allBtn);
  techs.forEach(t => {
    const b = document.createElement('button');
    b.className = 'hf-btn' + (historyFilter === t ? ' on' : '');
    b.textContent = TL[t] || t;
    b.onclick = () => { historyFilter = t; renderHistory(); };
    filtersEl.appendChild(b);
  });

  // Filter sessions
  const filtered = historyFilter === 'all' ? sess : sess.filter(s => s.tech === historyFilter);
  const sorted = [...filtered].reverse(); // most recent first

  body.innerHTML = '';
  if (sorted.length === 0) {
    empty.style.display = 'block';
    return;
  }
  empty.style.display = 'none';

  sorted.forEach(s => {
    const tr = document.createElement('tr');
    const durStr = s.dur > 0
      ? (s.dur >= 60 ? Math.round(s.dur/60) + ' min' : s.dur + ' sec')
      : '—';
    tr.innerHTML =
      '<td>' + (TL[s.tech] || s.tech) + '</td>' +
      '<td>' + s.date + '</td>' +
      '<td>' + (s.time || '—') + '</td>' +
      '<td>' + durStr + '</td>';
    body.appendChild(tr);
  });
}
